Identity review · 12 September 2026

Yidhan Mark Study

The current mark is a pleasant placeholder. It is on mood and off idea. It would not clear the first cut of a serious jury, and it says zen app where the product says private page. Three territories below fix that. A second round further down drops the assumptions all six shared, a third folds the best of them into one mark, and a fourth drops the kolam and keeps the warmth.

Is it award-winning?

No. Juries at D&AD, Brand New or the Type Directors Club look for one idea, drawn so well it can be owned. This mark has neither.

  • No idea. An arc over a dot reads, in order, as Wi‑Fi, a rainbow with a sun, an eyebrow, a gauge. Nothing in it says Yidhan.
  • Borrowed vocabulary. The brush arc and the ensō are the stock set of meditation and mindfulness apps. It cannot be owned because it is already shared.
  • No construction. It is a raster. It cannot be cut, embossed, engraved, animated or redrawn at another weight. That alone stops it at the door.
  • The look now reads as generated. Cream paper, a light serif, terracotta, a brush stroke: in 2026 this is the default output of image models, and increasingly people can tell. That works against “craft over convention”.

Does it match the ethos?

Half of it. It gets the temperature right and the meaning wrong.

  • Right: warmth, imperfection, natural pigment, quiet. A reflective writer would not be put off.
  • Wrong: nothing says private, yours, or offline. The product’s reason to exist is invisible in its mark.
  • Wrong: the name is Tamil and means Bright Spring. The mark is a Japanese calligraphy trope. The identity borrows a heritage the name does not have, and ignores the one it does.
  • Wrong: restraint. The brush tail on the right is a flourish, exactly the kind of element “that has not earned its place”.

Three territories

Each is a real idea, drawn as vector, tested at 16 px, and made so the two-colour figure survives Midnight: the ink follows the text colour, not the accent, and gold stays gold. The sketches are territories, not final marks. A final mark needs a week of drawing and optical correction in any of them.

A · Typographic

The Source

A Y is two streams meeting. Put the spring where they meet, and the initial becomes the mark.

Kintsugi
16 32 mono icon
Midnight
16 32 mono icon
Construction
A custom Y in the spirit of Cormorant: thick left arm, hairline right arm, a stem with a flat foot. The three strokes stop short of a single gold point. The point is the confluence, and the confluence is the spring. The wordmark is the same drawing continued: the Y is the mark, the mark is the first letter.
Why it clears
The idea lives inside the name, so no one else can have it. One asset does every job: header, favicon, app icon, print. It is a monogram, and monograms are the form juries trust most because there is nowhere to hide bad drawing.
Heritage
This is the territory where the Tamil name can finally show. The custom Y’s counter and the way the arms enter the stem can be informed by Tamil letterforms. Do that with a Tamil-literate type designer, not by pastiche.
Watch
The gold point must sit at the junction, never in the cup, or the Y reads as a glass with an olive. At 16 px the point becomes a dot of the same ink, which still reads as a Y.

Construction
The signature radius 2px 24px 4px 24px is already the most ownable thing in the product. Draw it as a single open stroke, one breath, one page, and leave the gap where the stroke fails to meet itself. The gold point sits in that gap. The gap is the imperfection. The gold is the repair. That is kintsugi, the name of the light theme, in one glyph.
Why it clears
It keeps what you have invested in (a hand stroke, terracotta and gold) and replaces what was borrowed. The logo and the interface share one geometry, so every note card on screen is a small repeat of the mark. A jury would call that a system, not a logo.
Finish
Draw the stroke with a real brush on real paper, scan it, and vectorise it as one path with optical corrections. Ship the vector; keep a textured version for print and the splash screens.
Watch
Too clean and it becomes a rounded rectangle, which is every app. The asymmetry and the stroke’s own tremor carry it, so do not straighten either. The gap must stay wide enough at 16 px to read as a gap.

Construction
A river stone in the card’s asymmetric geometry. Cut out of it, in negative, is a small note card in the same geometry, reached by a fissure from the top edge. The gold point marks where the fissure surfaces: the bright spring. The mark is what the server never sees. Tags and timestamps are the stone. The thought is the space inside it.
Why it clears
It is the only one of the three that carries the product’s core promise, and it does it without a padlock. Negative-space marks are the kind juries remember and write about, and this one has a story that is literally true of the architecture: encrypted content is the thing that exists only as absence.
Watch
Rounded shapes with an off-centre hole can read as a SIM card or a USB stick. The fissure and the asymmetry are what stop that; test it on strangers before committing. In mono the gold point folds into the ink and the mark still reads.

Round two

The six ideas so far, mine and Codex’s, share three assumptions: the mark is a symbol, it sits in the header, and it needs a shape. Each idea below drops one of them. They also stop borrowing from Japan. The name is Tamil, and Tamil has its own grammar of dots and lines that fits this product better than any ensō did.

Round two · 1 · The mark is an act, not a symbol

Kolam

Four dots and one unbroken line, drawn fresh at the threshold every morning and gone by evening.

Kintsugi
Yidhan
16 · pulli 32 mono icon
Midnight
Yidhan
16 · pulli 32 mono icon
Idea
A kolam is the rice-flour drawing Tamil households make at the doorstep each dawn: a grid of dots, the pulli, and a single line that loops around every one without breaking. It is drawn by hand, walked over by noon, and drawn again tomorrow. That is the product. A quiet home. A threshold. One line of thought that goes around everything. Notes that fade and are let go. The current logo is, by accident, a one-dot kolam drawn as an ensō. This draws it on purpose.
Construction
The smallest true kolam: a two-by-two grid of pulli in gold and one closed line in ink. Two lobes are drawn larger than the other two, the same 2 / 24 / 4 / 24 asymmetry as the note card, so it never settles into a clover. The line is a single path, so it can draw itself on launch and on the splash screen, then rest. At favicon size it reduces to its seed: one gold pulli. That reduction is the same element as idea 3 below, so the two are one system.
Why it clears
Nobody in this category has it, and no one outside Tamil culture can take it credibly. It is rooted where the name is rooted, which answers the heritage problem without inventing pseudo-Tamil glyphs. It moves, and the motion is the meaning, not decoration. Kolam have a formal grammar, so a later version can vary the line per device or per user within fixed rules, the way the MIT Media Lab and OCAD identities do, and a jury would write that down.
Watch
At 16 px a four-lobe loop is a blob; use the single pulli there. The line grammar has real rules, so bring in a kolam practitioner before anyone vectorises a “kolam-inspired” squiggle. The lobes must stay pinched, or it becomes a clover.
Round two · 2 · The mark lives in the margin, not the header

The Rubric

The oldest sign for “here a thought begins”, cut by hand in red and taken back from the toolbar.

Kintsugi
Yidhan
16 32 mono icon
Midnight
Yidhan

Idea
Before printing, a scribe left the paragraph marks blank and a rubricator came after, painting each capitulum in red. The mark meant a new thought and, later, a new chapter. Yidhan already has chapters, a manuscript glow, and terracotta. This identity takes the capitulum back as a hand-cut form and gives it a rule: it is always small, always in the margin, always red. The header logo is the one place it sits beside the name.
Construction
Yes, it descends from the pilcrow, and the point is to reclaim it. Custom drawing: a heavy bowl, two stems of unequal length, a crossbar that overshoots. Nothing about it should look typeset. In the app it becomes the paragraph mark, the section break, the empty-state glyph and the resting caret, so the brand is seen where the writer looks, not where the logo usually sits.
Why it clears
It is a literary imprint’s mark, not a wellness brand’s. It comes with a behaviour, which is what juries mean when they say “identity system”, and the behaviour is restraint: the logo is forbidden from taking the stage. One colour, one weight, survives 16 px, and prints in one pass.
Watch
The Word formatting-marks icon is the obvious collision. The custom drawing and the red carry the difference, so test the drawing against that icon at 16 px early. If it still reads as “show paragraph marks”, the idea is dead, not the drawing.
Round two · 3 · The mark does not need a shape

The Pulli

In Tamil script a dot above a letter silences it. One gold dot, above the name, and the discipline to stop there.

Kintsugi
16 · tile 32 mono icon
Midnight
16 · tile 32 mono icon
Idea
The pulli is the dot Tamil writing places above a consonant to take its vowel away. It is the mark of quiet. Yidhan’s gold seed has been sitting under an arc looking for a reason. This is the reason. The identity is the name in a light serif and one gold dot above it, at the place where the pulli would sit. The favicon is the dot alone on the forest tile, because gold on cream has no contrast at 16 px.
Construction
There is almost nothing to draw, so everything is in the rules: the dot’s size relative to the cap height, its position above the Y, its off-centre placement on the icon tile, and its behaviour on screen. It is the resting caret. It is the sync indicator when a note is server-confirmed. It is the only thing on the empty page. Everywhere else it is absent.
Why it clears
It is the bravest option in the category: a logo that is a single dot, with a real meaning behind it. Juries reward a brand that can hold its nerve, and every screen of the app already argues for this one. It keeps the founder’s gold. It costs nothing to render and nothing to scale.
Watch
A dot is only ownable through consistency, so the placement and size rules must be written down and enforced in code, not left to taste. On its own it is a full stop, so it should never appear without either the name or the behaviour that gives it meaning. If the kolam is chosen, this is its 16 px form, not a rival.

Round three

You asked for brightness, spring, the kolam and the Y in one mark. Four ideas in one mark is how logos become kitchen sinks, so the rule here is strict: each idea owns exactly one element, and no element carries two. The shape is the name. The line is the kolam. The dot is the brightness. Where the dot sits is the spring.

Round three · The synthesis

The Confluence

One unbroken line in the shape of the name. One point of light where the two streams meet.

Kintsugi
Yidhan
16 · pulli 32 mono icon
Midnight
Yidhan
16 · pulli 32 mono icon
Shape · the Y
The line traces the outline of a Y, not a typeset one: soft round terminals, a deep cup, arms shorter than the stem. It is the initial, so it can stand beside the name or replace its first letter, as the second lockup shows.
Line · the kolam
It is drawn as a kolam line: one closed stroke that never lifts and goes around the dot rather than through it. The cup dips toward the pulli the way a kolam line pinches between dots. On launch it draws itself from the foot upward, the way a spring rises and opens into two streams, then rests. Redraw replays it.
Dot · the brightness
One gold pulli, the Tamil dot of quiet, and the only colour in the mark. It is also the seed you already have. At 16 px the mark reduces to the pulli alone on the forest tile.
Position · the spring
A spring is the point where water surfaces and a Y is the point where two streams meet. The pulli sits at that confluence and nowhere else. Move it anywhere and the mark loses the word’s meaning, which is the test that it belongs there.
Why it clears
Nothing is stacked: remove any one of the four ideas and the form has no reason to be what it is. It is one path and one circle, so it scales, prints in one colour and animates for free. It reads as a Y from across the room and as a kolam to anyone who grew up with one.
Watch
An outlined Y with a dot is one step from a road-junction sign. The kolam hand keeps it away from that: the line must be drawn, not constructed, with the tremor of a real stroke and the slight lean of the arms kept. Get the cup depth and the pulli size right at 32 px before anything else. And the ய question still stands: the Tamil letter for “ya” is also three strokes from one root, and a Tamil-literate type designer may find the arms want to lean the way ய does.

Round four

Without the kolam the line no longer has to close, so the Y can be solid, calligraphic and warm. What is left is spring, brightness and the letter, and the way to make those three appealing rather than merely correct is to stop adding a dot to a Y. The light has to be a working part of the name.

Round four · Spring, brightness, Y

Yi

The light in the cup of the Y is the dot of the i. The mark spells the first syllable of the name.

Kintsugi
Yidhan
16 · sun 32 mono icon
Midnight
Yidhan
16 · sun 32 mono icon
The Y · spring
A spring is the point where water surfaces and two streams begin. The Y is drawn as a letter, not a stroke: a thick left arm with a flag serif, a hairline right arm, a stem with a flat foot, the Garamond logic of the wordmark, so the mark and the name share a hand. The first draft used three round-capped strokes, and it read as a figure with raised arms at every size. Serifs are what a body never has, so the letter reading wins. The water is in the confluence and in the motion, not in the arms.
The light · brightness
A gold sun rests in the cup, large enough to read as light in a valley rather than an olive in a glass. In the wordmark it is the tittle of the i, tucked under the right arm, and the i below it goes dotless. So the mark is not a Y with an ornament. It is Yi, the first syllable, and the brightness is the one dot the name needs anyway. Nothing added, nothing decorative.
Motion
On launch the stem rises, the arms settle onto it, and the light wells up out of the fork into the cup. Two seconds, once, then it rests. In print the same three elements sit still and mean the same thing.
Two readings
Across the room it is a valley at dawn: a bright spring as a place. Up close it is the initial. Marks that carry a letter and a picture at once are the ones juries remember, and both readings here come from the same three shapes.
Scale
At 16 px the sun alone, on the forest tile. At 32 px the full mark holds because it is three strokes and a circle. In one colour the sun becomes ink and the mark still reads as Yi.
Watch
The sun’s size is the whole game. Too small and it is a cocktail; too large and the Y becomes a cup. It must float between the arms, never sit on the fork, or the Y turns into a figure holding a ball. Draw it at 32 px first and fix the ratio there. The dotless i in the wordmark must be kerned tight under the right arm or the dot stops belonging to it. And with the kolam gone, the Tamil root now lives only in the name and its meaning; that is a fair trade for a warmer mark, but it is a trade.

Could the dot be a sun?

Yes, and the way it becomes one decides whether the mark stays calm. Four treatments, with the obvious one shown as the control.

Rays
The sun everyone draws, and the wrong one here. Eight rays add eight elements to a mark that has three strokes, they collide with the arms, they fuzz out at 32 px, and they are the sunrise glyph of every solar installer and yoga studio. Rejected, and shown so the reason is visible.
Hand disc
A drawn circle, not a compass circle. A slight wobble and a soft edge, the way a brush leaves a disc. It reads as a sun through warmth, it keeps the hand of the current mark, and it costs nothing at any size. This is the sun in every context.
Glow
On screen the disc carries a soft halo, the manuscript glow the editor already has. Print and favicon stay flat. The dot becomes light because it does what light does, not because it has spikes.
Lit Y
The sun lights the valley. The faces of the arms nearest the sun take gold and fade to ink at the tips and the foot. The sun is proven by its effect on the letter. It is the most beautiful of the four and the one that needs a rule: large sizes and the launch moment only, with the header keeping the flat version. In one colour it collapses to ink and still reads.
Recommendation
Hand disc always. Glow on screen. Lit Y for the splash, the landing page and print at size, where on launch the light wells up and then spreads along the arms. Never rays.

If you take one forward

  1. Pick a territory, not a sketch. My pick after four rounds is Yi: it carries the name’s letter, its meaning and its first syllable in three strokes and a circle, and it is the warmest thing on this page. The Rubric is the alternative if you want a literary imprint. The Y monograms are the safe fallback.
  2. Draw it as vector from the start. Three rounds of optical correction at 16, 30, 64 and 1024 px, on paper and on forest green, in one colour first. Gold is added last, never relied on.
  3. Make the ink follow text colour, not the accent, so the gold point survives Midnight. This fixes the current mark’s dark-theme collapse whichever direction you choose.
  4. Cut the assets from one SVG: favicon, PWA icons with a real maskable safe zone, splash screens, OG image. Retire the two masks, the Gemini sources and the orphan icons in the same commit.
  5. Record the decision in DECISIONS.md and add a changelog entry. The mark is a product surface.

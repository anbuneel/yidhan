A sprout under the sun

Round one's A, kept as three clear parts and redrawn to follow the Tamil letter இ more closely.

  • Sun arch. Brightness, for Idhanth. The arch and straight right stem of the letter.
  • Unfurling curl. Spring, for Ishya. It now rises on the right and turns left, as the letter does.
  • Two leaves. The wide, pointed loop at the foot of the letter, with the stem passing through it.

Both buttons change every mark on this page.

Decision 1

Filled or hollow leaves

The mark is drawn with one line weight. A hollow leaf at that weight leaves almost no space inside it, so there are only three honest ways to do it.

Filled Recommended

32 · 16

Reads as leaves at every size. The solid base grounds the mark, and the one solid shape against three lines is what makes it memorable.

Hollow, thinner line

32 · 16

Lightest and closest to the letter, which is all line. Costs: two line weights in one mark, and the leaves fade first at 16px.

Hollow, same line

32 · 16

Keeps one line weight, but the leaves have to get fat to leave any space inside. They start to read as loops, not leaves.

Decision 2

App icon: dark tile or sunrise seal

Android cuts every app icon to the launcher's shape, and installed web apps do the same. The icon has to survive a circle, a squircle and a rounded square.

Dark tile Recommended

The icon is the logo, not a second design. It splits cleanly into Android's two icon layers: an evergreen background and the mark in the safe zone. Its dark ground matches night mode. A faint marigold glow at the top keeps it from vanishing on dark wallpapers.

Sunrise seal

Louder on a home screen, but it is a circle inside a shape, and the mark has to shrink to fit inside the sun. Better kept for round places: a profile picture, a sticker, the share image.

Decision 3

The wordmark carries the writing

The mark says spring and light. The wordmark has to say "this is for writing". Book typefaces do that best. Each version swaps the dot of the i for a small marigold sun.

Fraunces, soft Recommended

Your question

Dark mode: yes, and make it the default

People write late at night, and the app opens in dark today. The new palette works in both modes. In the dark, marigold is the light: the caret, links and the main button. In the day, it stays a fill and never carries small text, because it is too pale to read on white.
